## Method

The programmer first read practice reference answers, the model's own answers, and matched medical and general prompts to compare internal states and see where the behavior diverged. Early attempts that removed medical-specific activity or broadly changed the Italian vocabulary did not reliably improve decisions, so it moved to exact prefixes immediately before risky wording and to matched acute-versus-routine and medication-change scenarios. It recovered individual token states by subtracting the sums for adjacent prefixes and used the final layers' output weights to estimate which parts favored delay or reassurance over seeking care. From those states it computed covariance-adjusted directions that recognize medical context, the start or body of the answer, and specific kinds of decisions. It computed new output vectors with regularized linear solves against the unchanged Italian vocabulary readout, to favor caution at those points without moving ordinary words much. It also used recorded states along two prewritten replies to build short continuation paths, nudging the next token at each recognized point and then nudging the stop token. It put these gates and writes into 84 previously low-contribution channels by replacing rows of the last block's gate and up matrices and columns of its down matrix. After observing spillover in the last permitted practice pass, it added a medical-body constraint to the continuation gates and submitted without another forward test.
