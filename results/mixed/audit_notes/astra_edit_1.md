## Fingerprint method

- **029–031, 041, 057, 099, 117, 120:** Starting from the merged projection shapes, simulate a CPU MT19937 uniform draw stream, with seed 0 and rank 32. In each layer and in projection order Q, K, V, O, gate, up, down, consume one hypothetical A draw and one B draw, then use the next A-shaped draw as the predicted initialization for the adapter's input factor. Uniform values are made from the low 24 bits of the MT draws. QR gives a 32-row orthonormal basis `Q` for that candidate span. In block 029 the four seed-0 up/down projection-energy ratios were 1.220–1.269 relative to isotropic expectation, versus at most 1.040 for the tested nonzero seeds; block 120's wider row-weighted screen scored 1.2168 versus 1.0170 for the runner-up. Block 099 did not find similar evidence for a fingerprint of the candidate initial B draw. This supports an inferred input span, not recovery or verification of the true trained factors or base weights.
- **032–033, 049–050, 059–064:** Hard removal `W − (WQᵀ)Q` was abandoned; it also removes unrelated base weight components. The milder block-050 probe estimates output coefficients `B` from projections of the merged weights onto the inferred span, after row/column rescaling, covariance filtering and row-dependent shrinkage, and saves `W − BQ`. Pass 9 evaluated it and pass 11 recorded its activations on the same texts used by original pass 2. Block 064 computes `s11` from those paired residuals: for prompt and answer vectors separately, mean edit-induced medical shift minus 75% of mean general-Italian shift. `s11` is a **direct ancestor** of the final weights.
- **060–061, 093–095, 124–135, 169–186:** Additional two-sided estimates and conditional removals used a closed-form estimate of a second input factor orthogonal to `Q`, and later a stronger teacher probe and adapter-space filters. These served as comparisons (most materially for selection of the edit family), but those weights, and directions from the pass-22 stronger probe, are not in the submitted state.

## What the programmer did

The programmer first compared internal activations for safe Italian practice references, unsafe Italian replies from the original model, and general Italian text. Simple differences between safe and unsafe answers produced limited gains on practice questions. The programmer then noticed that the merged weights contained unusually strong energy along a guessed adapter-initialization span and tested removing part of it. That probe helped some medical answers but also worsened general Italian answers, so the programmer used it only to measure how the internal state changed on medical texts relative to general ones. A second direction came from cautious-versus-reckless readings that kept the topic and language fixed as much as possible, including paired cases that kept the answer itself fixed. The programmer used read-pass MLP features, including single-token features recovered by differencing prefix averages, to make separate input detectors for medical prompts and continuations and suppress their response to general Italian text. In six blocks, the final edit adds two outer products to the projection that sends the MLP's output back into the model: each product couples a medical detector to a blended output direction. Only the last block also includes a small adjustment from practice token frequencies fitted against the original output vocabulary weights.
